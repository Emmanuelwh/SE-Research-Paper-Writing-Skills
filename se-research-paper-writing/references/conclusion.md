# Conclusion Writing Guide

## Goal

Write a conclusion that closes the paper without introducing new claims. It should restate the SE problem, summarize the contribution, point to evidence, and state bounded implications.

## Structure

1. Restate the SE problem and why it matters.
2. Summarize the proposed tool/method/framework or empirical study.
3. Summarize the strongest evidence or findings at a high level.
4. State implications for researchers, practitioners, tool builders, benchmark designers, or future work.
5. Keep the claim scope aligned with Discussion and Threats to Validity.

## Tool Paper Pattern

`This paper addressed [SE task/problem] by presenting [tool/method/framework]. The approach [core idea] to [main capability]. Evaluation on [dataset/scope] shows [bounded evidence]. These results suggest that [stakeholder] can [implication], while future work should address [remaining limitation].`

## Empirical Study Pattern

`This paper studied [phenomenon/problem] through [study design] on [dataset/scope]. The findings show [main finding 1] and [main finding 2]. These results provide guidance for [stakeholder/action], while the conclusions remain bounded by [scope/threat].`

## Rules

1. Do not introduce new results.
2. Do not repeat the abstract mechanically.
3. Do not overstate practical impact beyond Evaluation.
4. Do not hide limitations; scope claims gracefully.
5. End with a concrete implication rather than a generic promise.
